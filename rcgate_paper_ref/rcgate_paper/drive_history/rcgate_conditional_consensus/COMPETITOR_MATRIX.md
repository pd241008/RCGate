# Competitor matrix: incremental peer-response agreement

**Date:** 23 October 2026.  
**Question.** For a target RAG-generated sentence, does agreement information derived from other model responses to the same source/query provide incremental predictive information about that sentence's human-annotated hallucination span, beyond direct evidence-grounding signals; how does that increment vary by generator and task; and is it worth its additional compute?

**Paraphrase** restates a page opened on this date. **Inference** is this audit. The source-dependence STOP in `research/novelty_audit/` stands. The 23 October shift audit in `research/rcgate_shift_audit/` was read and not edited.

## 1. What would count as the same evaluation

A paper covers this question only if it has all of the following:

1. The unit is a sentence, or a span aligned to a sentence, from a RAG response.
2. The label is a human annotation of context hallucination, of the RAGTruth kind: the span is unsupported by or in conflict with the supplied source. World factuality is a different label.
3. One feature family is agreement, contradiction, or semantic distance against other models' responses to the same source and query. Resamples from the target model, and question paraphrases, are related but not the same input.
4. The comparison is nested. Evidence-grounding scores are fit alone, and the same examples are scored again with the peer-agreement features added. Separate horse races are not an incremental test.
5. Splits keep a source or query group on one side. Generator holdout removes that generator from training and threshold selection and does not train on the test sources through sibling responses. Task results are reported separately.
6. A peer-count or cost curve says what the extra responses buy.

No paper opened for this audit has all six. Several have two or three. That is enough to retire a new-method claim. It is not enough to say the measurement has already been reported.

## 3. Additional work checked because the required papers cite it

**SelfCheckGPT** (Manakul, Liusie, and Gales, arXiv:2303.08896, HTML v3 opened). The NLI variant scores a sentence by its contradiction probability against stochastic samples from the same generator. There is no retrieved-evidence premise and no RAGTruth span experiment in the sections read. Niu et al. and Bouke both use this family as a baseline. In Bouke's grounded span setting it is near chance, which is a warning about same-model resamples, not a measurement of the other five RAGTruth responses.

**HALT-RAG** and **CCUF** were opened in the 23 October shift audit and were not re-opened here. HALT-RAG fuses NLI models without a cross-generator panel. CCUF was abstract-only there. Neither supplies the missing cell.

A project blog for `firish/rag-rack` appeared in search. It reports a dual NLI ensemble on RAGTruth responses. It is not a paper, it does not use other models' answers as features, and its numbers are not used in this matrix.

## 4. Direct answer

The conditional incremental value of same-source peer-response agreement over target-sentence evidence-grounding, on RAGTruth-like human span labels, with leakage-safe source or query groups, separate held-out-generator and held-out-task results, and a peer-count cost analysis, has not been reported in the texts opened.

The pieces around that cell are occupied. Cross-model consistency without evidence is published. Evidence grounding without peer responses is published, including on RAGTruth spans. Changing an entailment score by ensemble disagreement is published on a different label. Comparing grounding and same-model consistency as alternative baselines is published, and in the one span table read the consistency baseline did not help. A new gating algorithm is not an open claim.
